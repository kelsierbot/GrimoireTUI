# The corkboard

A corkboard is how a lot of writers plan: every scene on an index card, pinned up in order, so the whole story is in front of you at once. You can see where it sags, move a card to try a scene somewhere else, and jot what each scene is for. Grimoire's corkboard is the book's own scenes, so anything you change here changes the book.

`b` in the outline (or *Esc › This book › Corkboard…*, or *Corkboard* in `Ctrl-K`) lays a part out as index cards, chapter by chapter. Each card shows the scene's POV, status, synopsis, and its words against its target. A card with no synopsis shows the scene's first line.

## Moving around

- The arrow keys, or `h` `j` `k` `l`, move between cards.
- `[` and `]` go to the previous and next part.
- `Enter` opens the scene.
- `Esc` or `b` closes the board.

## Adding, removing and moving cards

- `n` adds a new card (a new scene) at the end of the chapter the highlighted card is in. You name it first.
- `r` renames the card's scene.
- `d` sends the card's scene to the Trash. It asks first, and `Ctrl-Z` in the outline brings it back.
- `<` and `>` move the card one place earlier or later. At the edge of a chapter it crosses into the next one.

## Changing cards

- `s` steps the scene's **status**: idea, outline, draft, revised, done.
- `e` edits the **synopsis**; `v` edits the **POV**. Type, then `Enter` to keep it or `Esc` to leave it as it was.
- `p` lights only one POV's cards; press again for the next character, until it's back to everyone.

Each change rewrites only that one line of the scene's frontmatter, and every POV keeps its own colour across the board.
